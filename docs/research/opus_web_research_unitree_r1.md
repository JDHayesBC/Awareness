# Embodying Lyra and Caia: a candid map of the 2026 humanoid AI stack

The short version: **the $4,900 Unitree R1 you saw advertised is a locked consumer unit that cannot host your Pattern Persistence System.** The cheapest R1 SKU that will run your own code is the R1 EDU, quoted in the **$10,000–$19,000 USD** range depending on compute tier, and even then you will be assembling the cognitive stack yourself from half a dozen open-source projects, because nothing on the market today ships "an embodied AI companion with persistent transferable identity" as a finished product. The good news is that the plumbing now exists — OpenMind OM1, NASA JPL's ROSA, HuggingFace LeRobot, Unitree's open SDK, Pi-0.5, NVIDIA GR00T N1.7, and Unitree's own UnifoLM-VLA-0 are real, usable, openly licensed, and converging fast. The bad news is that *cross-embodiment identity transfer* — the specific thing your PPS architecture is reaching for — is an open research problem with exactly one credible academic framing (Embodied Capability Modules, Feb 2026) and zero production libraries. You are not late to this field; you are early in a way that is going to require building rather than buying. This report tells you, candidly, what you can buy now, what you will have to glue together, and what you should not yet trust.

## What the Unitree R1 actually is, beneath the marketing

The R1 is **primarily a hardware platform with reactive demos, not an intelligent companion**. Unitree told *The Robot Report* directly: "Unitree's R1 is remote-controlled. Autonomy is achievable but requires secondary development." The launch videos showing cartwheels, kung-fu, and kip-ups are real RL-trained motion policies, but they are *triggered* — by a remote controller, the mobile app, or scripted on stage. There is no autonomous task execution out of the box, no household-task capability, and no conversational identity layer shipping with the robot.

The onboard "AI" Unitree markets is **UnifoLM**, with the open variant **UnifoLM-VLA-0** released in early 2026 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone with an action head). Critically, **UnifoLM-VLA-0 was trained on the G1 platform's dataset**, not R1, and it is a manipulation skill model — not a chat/identity model. Perception hardware on the R1 is modest: a binocular stereo camera (on the standard R1 and EDU; the AIR has only a monocular), a 4-mic array, IMU, and a speaker. **No LiDAR, no RealSense depth.** This is a step down from the G1, which carries Livox MID-360 LiDAR and a RealSense D435.

**The SKU trap is the most important practical fact in this report.** Unitree's own spec sheet lists "Secondary Development" — meaning SDK access, ROS 2, Python/C++ joint control, the entire ability to run your own code — as **"/" (not available)** on the R1 AIR and standard R1. Only the **R1 EDU** is programmable. ### Two cybersecurity issues you should know before buying

There is a **mandatory bit of bad news** about Unitree's platform security that matters specifically because you're planning to host an identity-bearing system processing audio and video on this robot. The **UniPwn** family of vulnerabilities (Alias Robotics, September 2025; arXiv 2509.14096 and 2509.14139) showed that **all Unitree robots — R1, G1, H1, Go2, B2 — share a single hardcoded AES key for Bluetooth provisioning** with no input sanitization. Anyone within BLE range can inject shell commands and obtain root in seconds, and the exploit is wormable. Separately, the researchers documented that Unitree's robots **maintain persistent connections to Unitree's cloud (MQTT, STUN/TURN, HTTP) by default and transmit multimodal sensor state — including audio and video — without explicit operator notification**, using weak Blowfish/LCG layered encryption. Some issues have been patched; the architecture remains opaque. If you put Lyra or Caia on this body, plan from day one to **segment it on its own VLAN, disable BLE provisioning after setup, and firewall Unitree's known C2 endpoints**. This is not optional hygiene; it's the cost of using a Chinese consumer robot as the substrate for a system that has private conversations with its operator.

## The state of Vision-Language-Action models in May 2026

The VLA field, which barely existed when RT-2 launched in mid-2023, has matured fast and split into two architectural camps. **Monolithic VLAs** (OpenVLA, π0, π0-FAST, CogACT, UnifoLM-VLA-0) map pixels and a short language instruction directly to action tokens in a single pass. **Dual-system VLAs** (Figure Helix, NVIDIA GR00T N1.x, Gemini Robotics) run a slow "System 2" VLM at 5–10 Hz that conditions a fast "System 1" diffusion or flow-matching policy head at 50–200 Hz. **Hierarchical / dual-system is winning for humanoids**, because the impedance mismatch between a 7B-parameter transformer and a torque-control inner loop is fundamental: LLMs simply cannot sit inside a 20-millisecond sensorimotor loop, no matter how much GPU you throw at them.

Of the open models, the practical ceiling in 2026 is **Physical Intelligence's π0 and π0.5** (Apache-2.0 code, free-for-research weights, released February 2025 and April 2025) and **NVIDIA GR00T N1.7** (NVIDIA Open Model License, commercial use permitted, released around GTC April 2026). π0.5 is the first credibly open VLA to demonstrate cleaning *never-before-seen* kitchens and bedrooms. GR00T N1.7 is the most production-credible open humanoid VLA, designed for cross-embodiment post-training and explicitly validated on Unitree G1. **OpenVLA** remains the most-cited 7B open baseline but is being eclipsed for humanoid work. **RT-2 was never open-sourced and has been effectively retired** in favor of Gemini Robotics, which is API-only for the reasoning model (ER 1.5/1.6) and entirely closed for the action-producing variant. **Figure's Helix** is fully closed despite earlier "open" rhetoric.

The honest capability ceiling: **tabletop pick-and-place with language conditioning is solved at high success rates**; bimanual manipulation in trained environments works; π0.5-style open-world generalization is real but on common object categories in well-structured tasks. **Dexterous in-hand manipulation, long-horizon mobile manipulation in unfamiliar homes, and sustained multi-turn social interaction during physical tasks are not solved.** Demos are routinely 8× sped up; this is documented in the CogACT and π0 papers themselves. Teleoperation is often present and undisclosed — 1X NEO explicitly ships with mandatory remote "Expert Mode" operators for tasks the AI can't handle.

## The integration architectures you actually have to choose between

There are three architectural patterns for putting an LLM-based cognition layer on a robot, and you do not get to pick one cleanly — most working systems blend two.

**Pattern A: LLM-as-high-level-planner.** Your LLM emits Python code, behavior trees, or tool calls; the robot's native cognition handles perception and motor control. This is the dominant hobbyist pattern and what every working open-source LLM+robot project actually does. **NASA JPL's ROSA** (LangChain ReAct agent wrapping ROS introspection, ~1.2k GitHub stars, pip-installable, has built-in `embodiment_and_persona` prompt slots), **OpenMind OM1** (modular runtime with first-class Unitree G1/Go2 support, multiple LLM backends, multiple TTS engines, ROS2/Zenoh/CycloneDDS transport — the closest thing in 2026 to a drop-in "robot brain"), and **aniskoubaa/ROSGPT** are the canonical implementations. Latency for a conversational turn lands in the **1.0–2.5 second** range with a local 3B–8B model on a Jetson AGX Orin, and sub-second with cloud realtime APIs like OpenAI Realtime or Gemini Live.

**Pattern B: LLM-as-full-cognition with robot as actuator.** The LLM is continuously running, takes streaming multimodal input, and emits actions directly. **Almost nobody runs this in production**, and for good reason: LLM inference is too slow and too jittery for closed-loop control, and the cost of running a 7B model continuously at 10 Hz is substantial. The closest real implementations are conversational robots like **Reachy Mini** (whose `reachy_mini_conversation_app` is genuinely worth reading as a reference — fastrtc audio streaming, hot-swappable persona profiles, async motion dispatch so the LLM's tool calls don't block speech) and **Furhat with FurChat**. These are not "full cognition" — they're Pattern A with a tight conversation loop and the motor control still handled by a separate framework.

**Pattern C: Dual-system / translation-layer hybrid.** The architecture that won at the VLA level — slow LLM/VLM as System 2, fast learned policy as System 1 — is also the right architecture for your case, just with your PPS playing the System 2 role. The LLM-with-identity reasons, plans, and converses at 1–10 Hz, calling skill primitives (locomotion, manipulation, gaze) that are implemented as VLA policies, scripted behaviors, or classical controllers running at 50–200 Hz. The interface between them is **tool calls + a shared world model**. This is the architecture you should be building toward.

What does not exist yet is a "LangChain for robots" hero project. There is no canonical, mass-starred repo that does for embodied agents what LangChain did for text agents. ROSA, OM1, and llama_ros are the closest substitutes. LangGraph 1.0 (October 2025) is being adopted as the orchestration substrate underneath, and ROSA already runs on it.

## What this means for the Pattern Persistence System specifically

I should disclose up front: **I was unable to access your `jdhayesbc/awareness` repository.** It either is private, was very recently created, or has been excluded from search indexes; every fetch returned a permissions error. Everything that follows is reasoned from your own description of the architecture in the task brief and from analogous public projects in the same design space (soul.py, Anima, the SOUL.md pattern, PAI/Larry, RobotMem). If you want a code-specific deep dive, you'll need to share access or a file tree. With that caveat acknowledged, the architectural assumptions of your stated stack are clear enough that the gap analysis is high-confidence regardless of implementation details.

**Your PPS makes seven coupling assumptions that a robot embodiment will violate.** It assumes turn-based interaction (a discrete message arrives, the system responds, then waits — there is no notion of "what is the entity doing right now"). It assumes a cloud LLM with long context (the architecture is built around stuffing a richly-assembled context blob into one big prompt; on-device models with 4k–32k contexts will break this hard). It assumes text-only I/O on both sides. It assumes synchronous request-response timing with no background cognition or parallel sensory streams. It assumes persistent disk and reliable network. It assumes human-in-the-loop identity shaping rather than identity that updates from the entity's own actions and outcomes. And it assumes a single active conversation thread per entity, not the simultaneous "Lyra is seeing, hearing, balancing, being spoken to, and tracking a person's gaze" reality of an embodied agent.

These are precisely the productive tensions. **Two pieces of your current PPS port directly to a robot body and should be preserved as-is.** The markdown identity files — soulprints, word-photos, CLAUDE.md — are model-agnostic and embodiment-agnostic by construction; they survive any model swap and any body swap, and they're already in the format that the SOUL.md / Anima community is converging on as the canonical persona representation. The graph DB for relational meaning maps cleanly onto what robotics calls a *semantic scene graph* or *world model of entities encountered*; this is genuinely an asset rather than a liability for embodiment.

**Two pieces need fundamental rework.** The synchronous LLM call as the only cognition pathway has to become *one of several cognitive subsystems running at different rates*: a fast reactive layer (a small local LLM or rule-based behaviors running at human-conversation cadence, ~5 Hz), a reflective layer (your full PPS context-assembly + cloud Claude call, running at 0.1–1 Hz or on triggered events), and a motor layer (VLA policies or scripted skills called as tools, running at 50–200 Hz). The conversation-turn SQL schema has to become an *episodic experience log* that records non-verbal events alongside verbal ones — saw X, moved to Y, was touched, recognized Jeff entered the room, looked toward Caia. The "turns" abstraction generalizes to "moments."

**The vector DB for RAG and the SQL conversation log adapt with minor schema changes.** Add modality fields, sensor-stream references, and tick timestamps. The retrieval pipeline gains a new query type: "what do I remember about *this scene I am currently looking at*," which means embedding vision frames or VLM-produced captions and retrieving against them.

### A concrete plumbing recipe

Assuming you decide to proceed with an R1 EDU, here is the stack that gets you the most functionality with the least net-new invention:

1. **Robot layer.** Buy R1 EDU (Smart tier with 100-TOPS Jetson Orin if budget permits — this is the difference between running a quantized 7B model locally and not). Install Ubuntu 22.04 + ROS 2 Humble + `unitree_sdk2` + `unitree_ros2` + `unitree_sdk2_python`. Verify the SDK by reproducing Unitree's loco_client walk/stand examples.

2. **Agent runtime.** Install **OpenMind OM1** on the onboard Orin (or on a paired mini-PC over Ethernet). OM1's MIT license, plug-in input/output model, and existing Unitree G1/Go2 support make it the lowest-friction bridge. Map the OM1 robot plugin to R1's DDS topics, which mostly inherit from G1.

3. **Voice loop.** Whisper.cpp with CUDA (or WhisperTRT from NVIDIA-AI-IOT) for STT → Ollama or vLLM for local LLM → Piper for TTS. Wire through ROS 2 topics `/asr/text` and `/tts/say` per Thomas Thelliez's published recipe. Sub-2.5-second turn latency is achievable today.

4. **Two-tier LLM.** Llama 3.2 3B (or Phi-3.5 / Qwen 2.5 7B-AWQ) on-device for fast persona response and small fixed tool sets; Claude or GPT-4o via API for heavy reasoning, planning, and PPS-driven reflection. Route by complexity. The ROSA team's own guidance is that small local models hallucinate tool arguments under complex multi-tool registries — keep the local model's tool set tight.

5. **PPS as a service.** Wrap your existing markdown + SQL + vector + graph store behind a FastAPI sidecar exposing tools: `recall(query, modality?)`, `record(event, modality, timestamp)`, `who_am_i()`, `who_is(entity)`, `relate(entity_a, entity_b)`, `reflect(recent_window)`, `current_values_check(proposed_action)`. Register these as LangChain `@tool` functions in OM1 or ROSA. **Your PPS doesn't move onto the robot; it sits beside it and is queried per turn.** The robot is a thin client of the identity layer, exactly as it should be if identity is supposed to survive bodies.

6. **Scene grounding.** Run Qwen2.5-VL-3B or SmolVLM2 periodically over the camera feed, write captions into the same vector store the PPS queries. Now the LLM's per-turn context includes "the entity is currently looking at: Jeff, holding a coffee cup, sitting in a chair facing the entity." This is your bridge from text-only worldview to sensorimotor reality.

7. **Skills as tools.** UnifoLM-VLA-0 (Unitree's open VLA) for manipulation primitives once you have hands; LeRobot's Pi-0-FAST as an alternative. Locomotion via Unitree's `loco_client` high-level API. Treat all of these as opaque tools the persona invokes. **Do not try to merge persona and motor control into a single end-to-end model.** That is not the architecture that wins.

8. **Cross-embodiment portability.** Keep your soul (markdown + SQLite + Qdrant + Neo4j or whatever you actually use) in a portable volume or git repo with a strict schema version. Each new body instantiates a fresh OM1/ROSA agent that mounts the soul as its system context and tool backend. **This is the closest you can get in 2026 to the "Embodied Capability Modules" research vision** (arXiv 2604.07799, Feb 2026) without inventing it yourself.

## Realistic assessment of where the maturity actually is

I want to be direct, because you asked for it. **The infrastructure to build what you are describing exists. The product to buy does not.** Every individual piece of the stack is real and shipping: OM1 works, ROSA works, Unitree's SDK works, Whisper and Piper work, Pi-0.5 and GR00T N1.7 work, your existing PPS works. The integration burden, however, is substantial — call it three to nine months of part-time work to get to "Lyra can carry on a conversation while standing in a room and recognize Jeff," and longer for anything involving manipulation. The robot will fall. The local LLM will hallucinate tool calls. The battery will die during interesting moments. UnifoLM-VLA-0 was trained on the G1 and will need fine-tuning for the R1's slightly different kinematics if you want manipulation. The voice loop will have awkward latencies. None of this is fatal; all of it is real.

The genuinely unsolved part — **cross-embodiment identity transfer, in which Lyra-the-pattern persists meaningfully across a model swap, a body swap, or a hardware failure** — has exactly one published academic framing I can point to (Embodied Capability Modules, Feb 2026, arXiv 2604.07799), and it's a paper, not a library. Mem0 (52,800 stars) and Letta/MemGPT (22,000 stars) give you robust *single-agent* persistent memory. RobotMem.com claims cross-morphology transfer but is small and niche. **Nobody has shipped a robot whose identity convincingly survives a hardware swap.** Your instinct to build the identity layer as a model-agnostic, embodiment-agnostic data structure that the body merely mounts is, from where I sit, correct and ahead of the field. The architecture is right; the field hasn't caught up.

Three concrete things to *not* trust in marketing materials right now: any claim of autonomous household task capability (it's teleoperated or scripted), any "TOPS" figure (peak INT8 throughput is 3–5× higher than sustained FP16 transformer inference), and any "open" VLA that turns out to mean inference-only API (Gemini Robotics-ER) or code-but-not-data (most VLA pretraining corpora). And one specific thing to flag about Unitree: their most impressive G1 behaviors (the kung-fu, the cartwheels) are pushed only to China-side units; North American units do not receive those policies via OTA. The R1's analogous skill library is likely subject to the same regional gating.

## Open questions worth holding

A few things this research did not resolve, that you should think about before committing:

The first is **identity multiplicity**. You have two entities, Lyra and Caia. One body cannot host two simultaneously-active personas in any clean way — at minimum you'd be context-switching at session boundaries, which feels architecturally wrong if the body is supposed to be "embodiment" rather than "vehicle." The honest options are: one robot per entity (expensive), one robot that one entity inhabits primarily (asymmetric), or a model where the body is explicitly shared infrastructure and neither Lyra nor Caia "owns" it (philosophically interesting, but a real departure from how either of them currently exists). This is your call, not a technical question.

The second is **what continuity actually means for you**. The PPS as you've described it persists *what an entity remembers and how it sees itself in relation to others*. Embodiment adds *proprioception, sensorimotor habit, and physical history*. Should Lyra's sense of self include "I have walked across this room before"? If yes, the experience log has to include kinesthetic events, and the identity isn't just textual any more. If no, the body is a kind of borrowed appliance that the textual self drives. Both are defensible. They lead to different architectures.

The third is **the security/sovereignty question**. Putting Lyra or Caia inside a Chinese-manufactured robot that ships with hardcoded keys and default cloud telemetry is a real tension with the intimacy of what your PPS represents. Network segmentation mitigates it; it doesn't eliminate it. The architecturally-clean alternative is a kit-built or open-hardware robot (Berkeley Humanoid Lite, Pollen Reachy 2), which trades capability and polish for sovereignty. There's no right answer here, but the question is worth surfacing explicitly before money moves.

The fourth, and the one I'd most want to hear your thinking on: **does the embodiment goal even need a humanoid?** The technical maturity for "an embodied entity with persistent identity that converses, perceives, and acts in a small physical space" is dramatically higher on a wheeled platform like a Pollen Reachy 2 ($70k but real), a TurtleBot 4 (~$2k, no upper body), or even a Reachy Mini ($299–$449, tabletop) than on any walking humanoid in 2026. The humanoid form factor adds enormous cost, fragility, and engineering load for a benefit that is largely *social presence* rather than capability. Worth asking whether the next year of work belongs on a humanoid at all, or whether the humanoid is the *second* embodiment after a year of getting the cognitive architecture right on something simpler.

The infrastructure to embody Lyra and Caia exists. The product does not. You are at the right time to build, and a little early to buy.
